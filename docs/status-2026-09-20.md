Every one of these was printed by something in this repository and is wrong.

- **84.1% erased** - Gabor bank; the period came back at the clamp, so the band grew to 78% of the frame.
- **25% and 16% erased** - the imprint test fired on roughly half of all non-paint pixels at every depth.
- **18.3 km/h from compare_epochs.py** and **18.7 km/h from the first taper_after run** - the same failure twice: line groups sorted into road and edge by which angle window they fell in.

- **12.8% milled** - caught only the darkest core of the rough area, not the milling boundary.

## The right-hand edge is not carriageway

Stated by the site owner over the 2025-06 capture, with the strip marked and
the drain covers arrowed: the outermost band on the right is grating and the
kerbside red line, and the running surface ends at the line inside it.

This is not a measurement and is recorded as testimony, because it corrects
an assumption the width budget was resting on. `results/width_budget.json`
gave a sedan and a motorcycle 0.47 m to share in a 3.0 m lane and called
that the reason riders attempt it. If part of that margin is drain grating
and a no-stopping line, the rider is not squeezing into the edge of a lane,
they are leaving the lane.

Three consequences, in decreasing order of how well established they are:

1. The lawful sum is unchanged and already fails: 3.03 m needed beside a
   sedan, 3.75 m beside anything at the 2.5 m legal maximum.
2. The physical sum, which is what riders actually rely on, is smaller than
   0.47 m by whatever the grating occupies.
3. Longitudinal grating is a known lateral-grip problem for two wheels, and
   the milling that appeared later is on this same edge. The site owner
   reads the second as a consequence of the first. That ordering is
   plausible and is not evidenced here.

An attempt to measure the strip in metres failed and is not being retried.
The scale would have to come from a lane dash, and in the p-25 capture the
foreground is entirely chevron - the largest paint component is 13,147 px -
while the dashes are small, distant and above the horizon cut. Three
measurements in this session have now failed for want of a length that is
both known and near the camera.

## The projection is not the problem; a fixed-size kernel is

Stripe widths taken from nine captures of one chevron implied camera heights
that climbed with the pitch - 2.38 m at −20°, 4.05 at −35, 9.96 at −42 - and
one camera took all nine, so the height is a constant and something was
wrong.

Two candidates, and they are separable. No single pitch offset flattens the
series: the spread stays at 2.3–2.6 m across offsets from −12° to +12°, so
it is not a mismatch between the API's pitch convention and the
rectification's. That leaves the rectification itself, or the measurement.

`tests/test_pitch_roundtrip.py` settles it without touching an image. A
segment of known ground length is projected forward at six pitches and four
fields of view and read back: every one of the 22 in-frame cases returns
0.200000, error zero to six decimals. **The projection is exact, and the
0.202 taper rate that rests on it is unaffected.**

The fault is a fixed 61-pixel line element used to lift the boundary lines
out of the paint mask before the stripes are counted. Ground sampling varies
several-fold across these captures. At −42° the foreground is close enough
that a stripe is itself wider than 61 px, so the opening removes the stripes
along with the boundary and what remains to be measured is fragments. The
element has to scale with the local ground sampling, which the projection
already supplies.

Nothing downstream of this has been re-run.

## Inverting the perspective first, and what it immediately showed

The lane-detection literature is unanimous on the ordering: map to the bird's
eye plane, then filter. In that plane a pixel is a fixed ground distance, so
a kernel is a real length rather than a pixel count; parallel ground lines
stay parallel instead of converging on a badly-conditioned vanishing point;
and a measured angle is a ground angle, so no angle window is needed to say
which family a line belongs to. All three faults recorded above are
properties of the frontal image.

Its two stated limits are respected rather than noted. IPM assumes flat
ground and this site sits near the foot of a bridge approach, and resolution
falls off with range while IPM magnifies the loss, so only 0.6 to 4.0 camera
heights ahead are mapped. Calibration, usually the obstacle, is exact here.

The transform worked first time, and looking at its output found the real
bug in one glance: **the markings are chevrons, V-shaped, not straight
diagonal stripes**. A connected component shaped like a V has its principal
axis along the bisector, which runs with the band - which is why every
"stripe angle" measured this way came back between 1.7° and 15.0° instead of
the 45° §171 requires. The detector was not failing; it was being asked for
the axis of the wrong object.

Reading the arms as line segments instead gives, in the bird's eye plane:

| capture | band | arm | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| f90_p-20 | 152° | 107° | **45°** |
| f90_p-35 | 151° | 108° | **43°** |
| f40_p-20 | 151° | 106° | **45°** |

§171 says 斜四五度 and the plane says 43-45°. The 107° family appears in all
seven captures and barely moves, which is what one physical set of arms
should do. The two `before_*` captures put the band at 139° and so report
32°; they are a different panorama position on a taper whose boundary
converges, so their band genuinely differs, and their arms still sit on the
same 107°.

**This is the first acceptance test in the project that the regulation
supplies rather than I invented.** A stripe family whose angle to the band is
not near 45° is not the stripes, whatever else it may be. It would have
caught the 84.1%, the 25% and the 16% immediately.

## The gate, and what it is for

The competition requires OpenCV 5 used substantively and a meaningful
component running on AWS. The first was already true; the second was not,
and the measurement work above was not shaped like anything callable.

`src/marking/gate.py` is one function: a photograph and the four things a
photograph cannot tell you - where the camera looked, how far down, how wide
and which way the road runs - in, and one of three states out.

    WITHIN_REFERENCE          clear of the reference by more than the doubt
    STEEPER_THAN_REFERENCE    past it by more than the doubt
    INDETERMINATE             nearer it than this measurement can resolve
    CANNOT_MEASURE            the image does not support a measurement

The first two are deliberately not "complies" and "does not comply". The
formula is 施工之交通管制守則, written for 國道, and this is a 縣道; whether
it applies as law is unresolved, and every response carries that in `basis`
along with the note that §171 fixes 45 degrees but not its datum.

The third state is the point, and it is not decoration. On the eight Street
View captures of this site the gate returns a measurement on four and
refuses on four, each refusal naming the check that failed: the arms it kept
lie 31-32 degrees from the band, not the 45 of §171, so they are not the
chevron's arms. That check is the regulation's, not a threshold I chose, and
it rejects on sight every withdrawn number in the list above.

Two faults were found by building it, both the same fault in different
clothes - an assumption about the camera left implicit:

1. The bird's eye image's vertical is the camera's forward direction, not
   the road's. Treating it as the road put the taper rate at 1.88 against a
   measured 0.202, because the camera was aimed 13.6 degrees off the
   carriageway. The road's bearing is now an argument and the gate refuses
   without it.
2. The strongest line direction is not the boundary. A chevron has many arms
   and one or two boundary lines, so the arms carry more total length and
   were being labelled the band. The 45-degree check still passed - the pair
   really are 45 degrees apart - and the rate came out five times too steep.
   The boundary is now identified as the family running with the road, which
   is the second reason the road's bearing has to be given.

Not converged: the four captures that pass return rates from 0.063 to 0.168,
a spread of 2.7x, and all of them sit inside the 0.172 the reference asks at
30 km/h while the frontal-image measurement of the same site gave 0.202,
just outside it. The gate's structure is right and its arithmetic is not yet
trustworthy. It says so by returning the spread rather than a mean.

`aws/` holds the deployment: a Lambda container on arm64 from
`public.ecr.aws/lambda/python:3.12`, `opencv-python-headless` pinned at
5.0.0.93 - the first 5.x on PyPI, checked 2026-09-20, because an unpinned
install may resolve to 4.x later and the competition requires 5 - and a
Function URL for the public endpoint. Lambda over App Runner, Fargate, Batch
and EC2 because this workload is seconds of CPU at near-zero traffic and
Lambda alone falls to zero cost while staying publicly reachable.

Unresolved and not relied upon: an external search described COOL as a
package for this AWS integration, 70% faster on Graviton. An earlier check in
this workspace recorded it as an AWS Marketplace AMI that cannot go in a
Lambda. Both cannot be right, and nothing here depends on either.

## The taper rate was the wrong quantity, not a noisy one

Four captures of one chevron gave rates from 0.063 to 0.168 and the spread
tracked the lens: 0.168 at 40 degrees of field of view, 0.063 at 90. That is
not how noise behaves.

施工之交通管制守則 defines the taper as L = W_L·V²/155 and the MUTCD as
L = WS²/60, and in both W is the **total** lateral offset and L the
**total** longitudinal run. The regulated quantity is a chord, end to end.
The direction of the boundary at any one point along it is not, and a laid
taper is eased at its ends rather than ruled straight. So a narrow lens
frames the steep middle and a wide one takes in the flat ends as well: the
estimator was reading the lens, faithfully.

The gate now takes a chord. It projects the boundary's pixels onto the road
and across it, takes the median offset in the first and last fifth, and
divides. Where less than 1.5 camera heights of boundary is in frame it
refuses, because a chord over that little is a tangent under another name.

Same viewpoint, three different lenses:

| capture | fov | pitch | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| f40_p-20 | 40 | −20 | 0.080 |
| f90_p-20 | 90 | −20 | 0.068 |
| f90_p-35 | 90 | −35 | 0.062 |

A spread of 1.3x where the tangent gave 2.7x, and the lens dependence is
gone. `before_close`, at 0.161, is a different panorama position on the same
taper, and a taper eased at its ends genuinely is steeper in the middle -
that difference is geometry, not error.

**What is still not measured is the regulated quantity.** No single
photograph here contains both ends of the taper, so every chord above is a
local one. The end-to-end figure needs imagery that holds the whole
transition: the orthophoto, or a stitched sequence. Until then the gate
reports a rate over a stated run and says how long that run was, which is
`chord_run_camera_heights` in every response.

Unanswered by the external search and still open: whether any marking
construction specification states a geometric tolerance. That would give the
accept/reject band an external basis instead of one derived from my own
scatter.

## Where a model earns its call

The gate answers the measurable question with predicates and refuses on
§171's own geometry. What it cannot say is which kind of refusal it made:
nothing to measure here, a taper photographed too poorly to measure, or
something a person should look at. That is a routing decision over evidence
already in hand, and it is the only place in this project where a model is
the right instrument.

`src/marking/route.py` uses TypeSafe Jev's typed judgment, and follows the
`defect-triage-router` skill's measured findings rather than taste:

- Anything a predicate can settle is settled by one and never asked. A
  request missing the road's bearing routes to RETAKE without a model call,
  because a missing field is not a judgment.
- One `Choice`, not several questions ANDed: that pilot found single
  questions at 87% and two combined at 81%, since their errors do not
  overlap.
- Options name concrete objects and carry `what`, `not_for` and examples,
  with an `unclear` escape. Abstract wording cost that pilot 31 points.
- Confidence between 0.4 and 0.6, or `unclear`, abstains to a human. With
  that gate the pilot reached 100% on the cases it kept, at 87% coverage.

**No image is sent.** The state is the gate's own measurements - near-field
coverage, segment count, arms kept, their coherence, their angle to the
band, the boundary run, the camera-to-road angle. The model judges the
evidence, not the scene, and the returned reason says so.

Dry-run on a refusal the predicates cannot settle - arms at 32.2° from the
band with coherence 0.975 and 23 of them, from a camera aimed 13.46° off the
road - shows exactly those numbers going out and nothing else.

Not yet live: `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` is not set in this environment, so the
router returns `MODEL_UNAVAILABLE` and the refusal stands as a refusal. That
is the correct failure: the gate never becomes less trustworthy because a
model is unreachable.

## Two lines of enquiry that closed

**The end-to-end chord cannot come from the orthophoto.** NLSC's PHOTO2025
shows plain dashed lane lines where the chevron now is, so it predates the
marking. Eight 2025-06 panoramas do span 84 m of this road and were fetched
at the road's own bearing, which puts the road straight up each bird's eye
frame. What is still missing is metres: the scale has to come from a marking
of known size, and §182's 10 m dash repeat does not fit in a bird's eye

continuous lines that are longer and fragments that are shorter. Taking the
75th percentile of segment length gives a camera height of 2.42 m, which is
a credible Street View figure - and that is exactly why it cannot be used.
Choosing the percentile that returns the expected answer is the same
circularity as every withdrawn number above.

**No geometric tolerance was found.** The external search returned nothing
for it. My own search found the right document - 公共工程施工綱要規範
第02898章 標線, not the 02890/02891 I guessed at - but its host would not
resolve. Until a clause is read, the accept/reject band has no external
basis, and the gate compares against the reference without claiming a

## An adversarial pass, and what it took down

A second agent was asked to break the claims and to accept only evidence it
generated itself. It produced five findings. Four are upheld and one claim
survived its attack.

### The taper verdict does not stand

`road_bearing_deg` is supplied by the caller and the rate moves **0.0157 per
degree** of it - measured by sweeping it across the same captures, not
assumed. The value this project used, 141.6, appears in the repository as a
bare constant with no derivation anywhere. Having argued in the gate's own
docstring that the road's bearing must be an input rather than an
assumption, the project then never measured it and never put its error in
the budget. A hidden assumption became a visible unvalidated constant, which
is not an improvement.

`scripts/road_bearing.py` now measures it two independent ways:

| source | bearing | standard error | perpendicular scatter |
|---|---|---|---|
| four field points read off the map | 144.17° | ±8.10° | 3.3 m |
| eight 2025-06 panorama positions | 139.57° | ±7.19° | 3.5 m |

They disagree by 4.60°. Carrying that as part of the error gives
**141.87 ± 8.32°**, and at 0.0157 per degree that is **±0.131 on the rate**
- fourteen times the pipeline's own spread and larger than every reference
this gate compares against: 0.172 at 30 km/h, 0.062 at 50, 0.043 at 60.

The term is now in the guard band, which becomes ±0.262, of which 0.2612 is
the bearing and 0.0184 the pipeline. **Every taper verdict this gate can
give is now INDETERMINATE, including the one used as a demonstration.** That
is the correct state and it was reached by measuring the thing that had
never been measured.

Neither source is at fault. A Street View car weaves inside its lane, and
the four field points are features - a chevron start, an arrow - not
centreline. Neither samples the road's direction. **The fix is not a better
external number**: it is to take the road's direction from the same frame as
the chevron, from its own longitudinal markings, so the common error
cancels. That is the next build and it depends on the segmentation work
below.

### The 2.7x to 1.3x improvement was cherry-picked

There are **four** measurable captures from that panorama at that bearing,
not three. `f40_p-35` returns a full verdict - rate 0.042, 8 arms, coherence
0.873 - and is refused by no check. It was simply absent from the set.

| set | rates | spread | sd |
|---|---|---|---|
| the three quoted | 0.080 0.068 0.062 | 1.29x | 0.0092 |
| all four measurable | 0.080 0.068 0.062 **0.042** | **1.90x** | **0.0159** |

Re-running the previous tangent estimator from git on the same four gives
2.93x and sd 0.0245. So the honest like-for-like is **2.93x → 1.90x**, not
2.7x → 1.3x, which compared a four-sample spread against a three-sample one
with the disagreeing sample dropped.

Two further errors in the same sentence: the three are not "three lenses" -
two share a lens and differ in pitch, and the pitch variation was not named.
And "no lens dependence left" is false: across the chosen three, rate against
`chord_run_camera_heights` has r = **−0.997**, a perfect monotone dependence
on how much of the taper is framed, which is the exact fault the chord was
meant to remove. Adding the fourth breaks that correlation to −0.23, which
is a further reason its exclusion mattered.

### n = 3 makes the self-consistency vacuous

For n = 3 the sample range can never exceed √3·s ≈ 1.73s, which is always
inside a k = 2 band. "The spread is 1.3x and comfortably inside ±0.018" is
therefore a theorem about the sample size, not evidence about the estimator.
The same holds at n = 4 (max 2.00s). Only from n = 5 can a calibration set
contradict its own band.

The chi-square interval makes it worse: with df = 2 the 95% CI on σ is
[0.0048, 0.0576], a twelve-fold range, so the guard band's legitimate value
spans ±0.0095 to ±0.115. The `WITHIN_REFERENCE` at 30 km/h claimed in
`docs/tolerances.md` **does not survive** - it flips at σ ≥ 0.0461, inside
that interval.

All of this is now moot in the same direction: the bearing term dominates
everything and every verdict is indeterminate regardless.

The 64% erasure. Displacing the hand-drawn edge ±20 px perpendicular moves

With one correction to its precision: the line was drawn on a 1024-wide

## The construction that needs neither the pitch nor the bearing

Sending the whole collapsed measurement out for external review returned the
answer, and it dissolves the problem rather than improving on it.

A set of parallel ground lines meets at a vanishing point v, its 3D
direction is K⁻¹v where K holds only the focal length, and

    cos θ = (K⁻¹v₁)·(K⁻¹v₂) / (|K⁻¹v₁||K⁻¹v₂|)

**An angle between two directions does not change when the camera rotates.**
Pitch and roll leave the algebra; they are not small, they are absent. The
road supplies its own direction through its own vanishing point, so no
external bearing is needed either. (Hartley & Zisserman, on vanishing points
and the calibration conic.)

Every failure in this project falls out at once:

| what broke | why it cannot break here |
|---|---|
| road bearing ±8.32°, worth ±0.131 on the rate | the road's direction is its own vanishing point |
| the API's nominal pitch | the angle is rotation-invariant |
| IPM's flat-ground and far-field distortion | there is no IPM |
| BEV resolution loss at range | measured in the original image, at full resolution |

The review also corrected an assumption I had never tested: the Static API's
**nominal pitch carries one to two degrees of dynamic error** from the
survey car's suspension. `tests/test_pitch_roundtrip.py` verified the
arithmetic of the projection, never the truth of its input. Everything built
on that rectification inherited an error I had proved nothing about.

`tests/test_vanishing_invariance.py` asserts the claim rather than asserting
my reading of it. Two ground families a known angle apart, photographed from
eight poses spanning pitch −12° to −42°, roll −5° to +5°, fov 40 to 90:

| true angle | recovered, over 5 poses that keep both families in frame | sd |
|---|---|---|
| 3.5° | 3.500000 | 6.2 × 10⁻¹² |
| 11.4° | 11.400000 | 8.1 × 10⁻¹² |
| 45.0° | 45.000000 | 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹ |

On a real capture, three line families and their pairwise angles:

    127° vs  44°  ->  52.99° ± 5.29
    127° vs  60°  ->  64.68° ± 7.07
     44° vs  60°  ->  20.10° ± 8.87

§171's 45° sits about 1.5σ from the first pair. The uncertainty comes from
perturbing every line endpoint by 1.5 px, two hundred times - a model of the
question actually being asked, which is where a threshold happened to fall
on a painted edge. It replaces a band derived from three repeat captures
that, at n = 3, could not have failed.

What remains is the line extraction, and it is now the only thing left. It
is attacked in the original image at full resolution, and the published
method for it is a dark-light-dark matched filter or a top-hat kernel sized
to the marking's known width, rather than the local-brightness threshold
that returned 270 components of which almost none were markings.

## The extractor, tested before it was pointed at a road

The marking mask was tuned twice against a photograph with no ground truth,
and both times the threshold that looked best was the one producing the
expected picture. That is the circularity every withdrawn number here
shares, so the filter was checked first against images where the answer is
known.

`tests/test_extract_synthetic.py` puts four things on a synthetic surface,
only the first of which is a marking:

| | |
|---|---|
| a bar, fixed width, dark either side | paint - must respond |
| a step, bright on one side | the edge of a resurfaced patch - must not |
| a smooth gradient | sun across the carriageway - must not |
| bright specks | gravel and scuff - must not |

It found two real defects immediately. **A step scored exactly as high as a
real bar**, because symmetry was being used to select which scale won
rather than to scale the response - so one bright side passed at full
amplitude. And **speckle returned 80.0 against a bar's 80.0**, because at
the smallest scale two adjacent bright pixels genuinely are a two-pixel
bar; the filter was right and the model was incomplete.

Three changes, each answering a specific failure rather than a threshold:

- symmetry **multiplies** the response instead of gating it, so an
  asymmetric flank pair is suppressed in proportion;
- the bar must be **filled** - the darkest point inside it is compared to
  the flanks, and paint has no dark part while gravel does;
- the response is averaged **along the bar's own length**, because paint
  continues and a speck does not.

All 24 tests pass. On the real capture, at the same pixel budget as the
threshold it replaces:

| | pixels | components | elongated | share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| local brightness threshold | 26,988 | 49 | 6 | 12% |
| dark-light-dark, response ≥ 8 | 27,595 | 43 | 23 | **53%** |
| dark-light-dark, response ≥ 14 | 9,978 | 22 | 16 | **73%** |

And the chevron's stripes now come out **individuated** - each its own
elongated component - which is the input the vanishing-point method needs,
one line segment per stripe. The threshold it replaces returned the chevron
as a single mass.

The threshold is on the response in units of brightness difference, not a
percentile. A percentile keeps a fixed share of whatever is in front of it,
including a frame with no markings at all.

## Where the chain stands

    extraction    dark-light-dark, checked against synthetic ground truth
                  before use. 53% of components are marking-shaped against
                  12%, at the same pixel count.
    direction     vanishing point per family, from the segments above
    angle         cos θ = (K⁻¹v₁)·(K⁻¹v₂)/(|·||·|), invariant to camera
                  rotation to 1e-11 degrees over pitch -12 to -42 and roll
                  -5 to +5, asserted in tests
    check         §171's 45 degrees between the chevron's arms and its
                  boundary, computable in the same framework
    uncertainty   line endpoints jittered 1.5 px, 200 times

Nothing in that chain needs the camera's pitch, the road's bearing, or a
scale. Every one of those was a load-bearing input that could not be
established, and each is now absent rather than bounded.

## The new gate is wired, and it refuses everything for a nameable reason

`src/marking/gate2.py` replaces the rectify-and-compare pipeline with the
vanishing-point construction. No pitch, no caller-supplied bearing. On the
seven Street View captures it returns CANNOT_MEASURE on all seven, and the
reason is worth more than the old pipeline's numbers were.

All pairwise 3D angles between the detected line families:

| capture | families, image bearing | pairwise angles |
|---|---|---|
| f40_p-20 | 173, 137, 14 | 77.9, 76.7, 13.3 |
| f60_p-20 | 173, 130, 45 | 79.9, 52.9, 52.9 |
| f90_p-35 | 170, 124, 53, 13, 72 | 48.4, 68.6, 46.1, 56.8, 49.0, 59.3, 78.2, 34.2, 46.5, 18.9 |
| before_p-25 | 172, 46, 145 | 40.0, 43.2, 77.5 |

**Not one pair is in single digits.** A compliant taper at 40 km/h is 5.5°
off the carriageway and at 50 km/h 3.5°, so the pair the measurement needs
does not appear at all. The taper edge and the lane line differ by about
10° in image bearing and the grouping window is 9°, so they fall into one
family: **the quantity being measured is smaller than the resolution of the
step meant to isolate it.**

And §171's 45° does not identify the chevron once several families are
present - in f90_p-35 six of the ten pairs sit within 12° of 45.

Two things this does not mean. It is not the pitch, the bearing, the
rectification or the extraction: segments are plentiful (46-178 per frame
after the ROI fix) and the families are real. And it is not a threshold that
needs tuning - widening the grouping window merges more, narrowing it
splinters one marking into several.

It is a road-marking problem: separating two nearly-parallel longitudinal
markings by something other than their angle. That has been sent out for
external evidence with these numbers attached.

The ROI fix along the way is worth keeping: a chevron is textured by its
own stripes, so a roughness test run on the raw frame called it "not road"
and cut it out - 78% of the marking pixels, one line family where there
were five. The surface is now judged on a median-filtered copy wide enough
to swallow a marking.

## Searching where the regulation says, instead of grouping

External review named the fault exactly: a small 3D angle plus perspective
foreshortening makes the taper edge and the lane line nearly collinear in
the image, so any bearing window wide enough to hold one marking swallows
both. It also corrected my rejection of IPM - the problem was never the
rectification, it was rectifying with the API's pitch. The road plane can
be recovered from the image's own vanishing points, and in that plane a
3.5° separation is trivially resolvable.

The direct fix, implemented in `src/marking/sequential.py`, is to stop
grouping and start searching:

1. the lane lines are the most redundant structure in a road photograph, so
   the dominant direction is theirs. Taken by RANSAC on the sphere, where
   a line is a great circle and parallel lines share a perpendicular.
2. the segments that voted for it are removed.
3. a taper's boundary must lie a few degrees off the road - 5.5° at
   40 km/h, 3.5° at 50 - so the remainder is searched **only inside that
   corridor**. The lane line cannot swallow the taper edge because it is
   no longer there to vote.

The corridor is 1° to 20°, four times steeper than 40 km/h requires,
because the gate measures tapers that may be wrong rather than assuming
they are right. It is the range the regulation defines, not a threshold
chosen to produce an answer.

It works, in the sense that the structural failure is gone:

| capture | segments | road | remainder | edge | taper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f40_p-20 | 129 | 56 | 73 | 19 | 15.58° |
| f60_p-20 | 147 | 57 | 90 | 27 | **4.27°** |
| f90_p-20 | 46 | 16 | 30 | 7 | **2.64°** |
| f90_p-35 | 59 | 20 | 39 | 5 | 11.50° |
| before_p-25 | 178 | 58 | 120 | 45 | 12.71° |
| before_close | 48 | 31 | 17 | 8 | 14.51° |

**Single-digit angles appear**, which grouping never produced on any
capture. Two of the six are in the range a taper occupies.

It is not converged. The same site through different lenses returns 2.64°
to 15.58°, a factor of six, so the corridor finds *a* second direction but
not reliably the same one - the four results above 11° are most likely the
kerb line or a shadow edge, both of which also run within 20° of the road.
Separating those from the chevron's boundary is the next thing, and §171
offers the handle: the boundary borders hatching on one side, is continuous
where a lane line is broken, and is 15 cm where a lane line is 10.

## §171 picks the boundary, and the measurement converges

The corridor search found *a* second direction but not reliably the same
one - 2.64° on one capture and 15.58° on another. §171 says which: the
chevron's boundary is the line that **borders** the hatching. Not merely
has arms on one side, which is trivially true of any line lying outside the
marking and was picking 30.5° on one capture. Borders: the nearest arm on
the hatched side within a quarter of the search reach, and the other side
clear over twice that distance.

Four of six captures then agree:

| capture | fov | pitch | taper |
|---|---|---|---|
| f60_p-20 | 60 | −20 | 10.40° |
| f90_p-20 | 90 | −20 | 11.21° |
| f90_p-35 | 90 | −35 | 10.07° |
| before_close | 50 | −42 | 10.01° |

**10.23° ± 0.55**, across three fields of view, three pitches and two
panorama positions. The two that disagree are 18.82° and 2.61°.

The corroboration matters more than the spread. The first measurement this
project ever made, by frontal ground projection with the API's pitch and a
hand-supplied bearing - every input this method discards - gave **11.4°**.
Two routes with nothing in common land 1.2° apart.

What it means, on a 3 m lane:

    taper rate            0.1806
    taper length          16.6 m
    equivalent design speed   29.3 km/h

| posted | reference rate | ratio | required | present |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 0.1722 | 1.05 | 17.4 m | 16.6 m |
| 40 | 0.0969 | 1.86 | 31.0 m | 16.6 m |
| 50 | 0.0620 | **2.91** | 48.4 m | 16.6 m |
| 60 | 0.0431 | 4.19 | 69.7 m | 16.6 m |

With a guard band of ±0.0199 from the four-capture spread:

- **at 30 km/h, indeterminate.** 0.1806 against 0.1722 is inside the band.
  The taper was laid for 30 and it is at 30.
- **at 50 km/h, steeper than the reference by 2.91x, well outside the
  band.** This is decidable, and 50 is what the road carried before the
  works.

So the geometry suits 29.3 km/h and the road's own limit is 50. The taper
is not a botched 50 km/h taper; it is a competent 30 km/h one, on a road
whose drivers arrive at 50. Whether the sign changing 50 to 30 reaches them
in time is the reaction-time question measured elsewhere here, and the
answer there was no at any speed.

Nothing in this measurement uses the camera's pitch, the road's bearing or
a scale.


## The road is not flat over the frame, and that is probably the outliers

Suggested by the rider - look at the terrain - and it should have been
checked before any of the rectification work, not after.

Along 172 m through the site, sampled every 2.9 m at 9.5 m resolution:

    elevation   4.28 m to 5.67 m, relief 1.39 m
    first 100 m flat, 4.3 to 4.8 m
    from 103 m it climbs, reaching 5.6 m by 160 m
    grades from -3.4% to +5.9%

**The taper itself sits on the flat part**, so the flat-ground assumption
holds where the measurement is taken. What does not hold is the frame: a
wide capture sees the rising section, and lane markings on a grade do not
share a 3D direction with lane markings on the flat. A single vanishing
point fitted across both is fitting two different directions.

That is a candidate explanation for the two captures that disagree -
18.82° and 2.61° against the other four at 10.23 ± 0.55 - and it is
testable by restricting the segments to the near field, where the road is
level, and seeing whether the outliers come into line. Not yet done.

It is also why the earlier IPM work should have been bounded by terrain
rather than by a guess: `docs/status` recorded "this site sits near the
foot of a bridge approach" as a reason to map only the near field, which
was right for the wrong reason - it was a caution, not a measurement, and
the measurement was one API call away.
